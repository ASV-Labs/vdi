# 01_Scripting_and_Boards — Scripting and boards

How to write a short-form script and the board the performer reads from, so every line can be said in one breath, captioned in two short lines, proven on screen, and read without truncation.

Stages: script.

## What this topic covers

- SAY / DO / SHOW beats
- Sentence length and breath units
- Boards and teleprompters (and how they fail)
- Claim lists: spoken claim -> screen line -> run
- Ad-libs and the accepted-deviation list
- Proper-noun lists for review by ear

# Overview

## The short version

- **The performer reads what is in front of them.** On AX the board tool cut each SAY at three wrapped lines and added an ellipsis; the presenter read the boards exactly, so 18 lines across five shorts stopped mid-sentence and needed pickups. Whatever surface the performer reads is the script in practice. [AX ax-boards: board header note] [AX lessons-001: 1.1]
- **One idea per beat, one breath per sentence.** Long SAY lines were the ones that got truncated, rushed and mis-captioned on AX. Short sentences are easier to say twice, cut, pick up and caption. [AX lessons-001: 1.2] The ~12-word ceiling is a house rule. [UNVERIFIED]
- **Every beat names its screen.** A beat without a SHOW line produced screen time with nothing that proved the words (AX-01 'tested templates' had no templates on screen, 20.1-23.0 s). [AX ax-v5: AX-01 20.1-23.0 s]
- **What he says wins over what was written.** Ad-libs happen ('an Army soldier', 'calls back a design contract', 'Probably doesn't exist.'). The script is the intent; the final audio is the truth; the owner decides which deviations stand. [AX lessons-001: 1.3] [AX ax-reviewer: script vs spoken]
